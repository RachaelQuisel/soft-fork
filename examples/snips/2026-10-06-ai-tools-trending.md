### Snip 1: Kimi K2.6's agent swarms run for days, and expose the orchestration gap
- Source: VentureBeat (fresh analysis, fetched 2026-10-05), plus launch coverage from aiunderstanding.org and SiliconANGLE
- Episode/article: "Kimi K2.6 runs agents for days, and exposes the limits of enterprise orchestration"
- Date: 2026-10-04/05 (VentureBeat piece crawled ~12h before fetch; model released earlier in 2026)
- Link: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/kimi-k2-6-runs-agents-for-days-and-exposes-the-limits-of-enterprise-orchestration
- Summary: Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.6 is an open-weights model (1T-parameter MoE, 384 experts with 8 active per token, SwiGLU activation, MLA attention, 400M-param vision encoder) that scores 58.6% on SWE-Bench Pro, ahead of GPT-5.4 (57.7%) and Claude Opus 4.6 (53.4%), and is ranked the top open-weights model at #4 overall by Artificial Analysis. The newsworthy part is the orchestration model: "Agent Swarms" coordinating up to 300 sub-agents across 4,000 coordinated steps, with internal runs lasting hours and, in one case, five straight days of autonomous monitoring and incident response. Launch demos claim a full SysY compiler built from scratch in 10 hours (all 140 functional tests passing, characterized as four engineers over two months) and a 13-hour overhaul of an eight-year-old financial matching engine (1,000+ tool calls, 4,000+ lines changed). Unlike Claude Code and Codex, which orchestrate via a lead agent with user-defined roles, K2.6 lets the model itself determine the orchestration. Practitioners say the brittleness runs deeper than prompting can fix: maintaining state as the environment changes while the agent calls tools, APIs, and databases over hours is the hard problem. F5's CPO frames it as an architectural shift: "agents as persistent infrastructure" creating categories we lack names for, agent runtime, agent gateway, agent identity provider, agent mesh. Available on Hugging Face, Moonshot's API, Kimi Code, and the Kimi app.
- Loop/workflow angle: this is the open-weights answer to Oct 5's governance episode. Two things matter for Rachael's consulting: (1) the trust question flips, when the model does its own orchestration instead of following predefined roles, "who decided the plan" becomes unauditable, which makes the Authority Card pattern from yesterday's workshop even more load-bearing; (2) for clients who cannot or will not route work through closed APIs, a frontier-class open model that runs multi-day agent swarms changes the build-vs-buy math on long-horizon automation. Watch the "agent mesh" vocabulary, clients will start asking for it by name.
